# Mwendo Pamoja SPV Financial Model Architecture

## 1. Executive Summary

The Mwendo Pamoja Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) financial model is a sophisticated structured finance tool designed to evaluate the securitization of small-ticket, alternative-data-underwritten loan receivables. This document serves as the comprehensive guide to understanding the financial logic, mathematical relationships, and structural protections embedded within the model.

At its core, the SPV is a ring-fenced legal entity. Its sole purpose is to purchase loans originating from the Mwendo Pamoja platform (in Kenyan Shillings - KES) and to service the debt raised from international lenders (in US Dollars - USD) used to fund those purchases. The model's primary objective is to demonstrate to senior and mezzanine debt investors that the cash generated by the underlying loan portfolio will be sufficient to meet all debt obligations, even under severe macroeconomic or operational stress scenarios.

## 2. Asset-Backed Securitization (ABS) Principles

Securitization is the process of transforming illiquid assets (in this case, individual microloans, revolving credit lines, and insurance premium finance loans) into tradable securities. The Mwendo Pamoja model employs several key ABS principles:

*   **Bankruptcy Remoteness:** The SPV is legally separate from the parent operating company. If the parent company goes bankrupt, the assets within the SPV are shielded and continue to generate cash for the lenders.
*   **Tranching:** The capital structure is divided into layers (tranches) of varying risk and return. Senior debt takes the lowest risk, while equity absorbs the first losses.
*   **Credit Enhancement:** Structural protections, such as Overcollateralization (OC) and Cash Reserve Accounts (CRA), are implemented to artificially boost the credit quality of the senior debt beyond the raw credit quality of the underlying loans.
*   **Asset-Liability Matching:** The model attempts to align the duration and cash flow profile of the short-term KES receivables with the amortization profile of the USD debt.

## 3. The Asset Pipeline (Origination and Vintages)

The asset side of the balance sheet is driven by the `Origination_Drivers` and the product-specific cohort schedules.

### 3.1. Origination Drivers
The model does not rely on a static portfolio; it dynamically projects future originations. 
*   **Active Drivers:** The user base starts at 12,000 drivers and grows at a projected 4% per month.
*   **Product Mix:** The portfolio is diversified across three distinct products: Insurance Premium Finance (IPF) at 35%, Microloans at 40%, and Revolving Credit at 25%.
*   **SPV Purchase Share:** The SPV does not buy 100% of the parent company's originations. It only purchases 85%, leaving a portion on the parent's balance sheet (often required for regulatory "skin-in-the-game" rules).

### 3.2. Cohort Vintage Modeling
The most critical feature of the asset pipeline is its use of **cohort schedules**. Instead of modeling the portfolio as one aggregate pool (which obscures the timing of defaults and recoveries), the model tracks each month's originations as a distinct "vintage."

*   **Amortization:** Each vintage has a scheduled principal collection rate (modeled here at a flat 20% per month, implying a 5-month average duration).
*   **Default Curves:** Different products exhibit different risk profiles. IPF has a base default rate of 3.2%, Microloans 4.5%, and Revolvers 5.5%. These defaults are applied against the original cohort balance.
*   **Recoveries:** A portion of defaulted loans is recovered over time (assumed at 40% net recovery).

The `Collections_Losses` tab acts as the aggregator, summing the performance of all 36 monthly cohorts across all three products to produce the total KES cash flow generated by the portfolio in any given month.

## 4. Capital Structure (The Liability Stack)

To fund the purchase of the KES receivables, the SPV raises $9,000,000 USD, tranched into three classes.

### 4.1. Class A (Senior Debt)
*   **Size:** 75% of total capital ($6.75M).
*   **Advance Rate:** The 75% sizing corresponds to a 75% Advance Rate. For every $100 of eligible KES receivables (converted to USD), the SPV can borrow a maximum of $75 in Class A debt.
*   **Pricing:** Priced at a spread over a base rate (e.g., T-Bill + 200 bps).
*   **Risk Profile:** Safest tranche. Protected by the equity and mezzanine layers.

### 4.2. Class B (Mezzanine Debt)
*   **Size:** 15% of total capital ($1.35M).
*   **Pricing:** Fixed at a higher rate (e.g., 20% annualized) to compensate for increased risk.
*   **Risk Profile:** Subordinated to Class A, but senior to Class C.

### 4.3. Class C (First-Loss Equity / Subordinated Notes)
*   **Size:** 10% of total capital ($0.90M).
*   **Pricing:** Uncapped residual return. 
*   **Risk Profile:** This is the sponsor's capital. Any losses in the underlying KES portfolio immediately erode the value of Class C before affecting Class B or Class A.

## 5. Cash Flow Dynamics and the Waterfall

The `SPV_Cashflow` and `Waterfall` tabs dictate the strict, legally binding priority of payments. This ensures cash is not trapped or distributed improperly.

### 5.1. SPV Cash Flow (KES)
Every month, the SPV tracks KES inflows (Principal, Interest, Recoveries) and outflows (New originations purchased from the parent, Servicing fees, Trustee costs). The net result is the "Cash Available for Debt Service."

### 5.2. The Priority of Payments (The Waterfall)
Cash is distributed in the following strict order. If cash runs out at step 4, steps 5 through 10 receive nothing.
1.  **Statutory / Trustee Costs:** Ensures the SPV remains legally compliant.
2.  **Servicing Fees:** Paid to the parent company to ensure they continue collecting the loans.
3.  **Class A Interest:** Current interest due to senior lenders.
4.  **Class A Principal:** Scheduled or sweep principal due to senior lenders.
5.  **Class B Interest:** Current interest due to mezzanine lenders.
6.  **Class B Principal:** Principal due to mezzanine lenders.
7.  **Reserve Replenishment:** Topping up the Cash Reserve Account (CRA) if it has been drawn down.
8.  **Senior Sweep (Early Amortization):** If a covenant is breached, all remaining cash bypasses equity and is swept to pay down Class A principal.
9.  **Class B Sweep:** Similar to the senior sweep, applied to mezzanine if applicable.
10. **Class C Residual:** The leftover cash distributed to the sponsor equity.

### 5.3. Foreign Exchange (FX) Translation
A critical element of emerging market securitizations is FX risk. The SPV collects KES but owes USD. The Waterfall must flawlessly translate the USD debt service obligations into KES equivalents (using the current exchange rate) before subtracting them from the KES cash available. Failure to properly hedge or model this translation can result in severe shortfalls.

## 6. Credit Enhancements and Covenants

The model incorporates triggers to protect lenders.

### 6.1. Overcollateralization (OC)
The OC ratio is calculated as `Eligible Receivables / Secured Debt`. If the target is 1.25x, the SPV must maintain $1.25 of receivables for every $1.00 of debt. If defaults cause the OC ratio to fall below 1.25x, it triggers a "Reserve Cure" or Early Amortization, trapping cash to pay down debt until the ratio is restored.

### 6.2. Performance Covenants
The `Covenants` tab monitors the Non-Performing Loan (NPL) ratio. If the NPL ratio exceeds the defined threshold (e.g., 6.5%), the SPV enters Early Amortization. No further originations are purchased, and all cash is used to rapidly de-lever the SPV.

## 7. Insuretech Overlays and Bayesian Underwriting

What makes the Mwendo Pamoja model unique is its integration of telematics and alternative data, captured in the `Partner_Economics` tab.

Instead of traditional FICO scores, the underwriting engine utilizes "Explicit Liquidity Features."
*   **Telematics:** Hard braking (measured by IMU) or circadian fatigue indicators act as early warning signals for potential defaults or insurance claims.
*   **Wallet Ledger Data:** Earnings Velocity and Repayment Velocity provide real-time visibility into the borrower's cash flow.

Governed neural representations and separately owned explicit liquidity features feed the hierarchical Bayesian model without duplicating the named engineered metrics. The downstream Credit Policy and Compliance Gate can then restrict routing, alter credit limits, or trigger restructuring before a loan formally defaults. The SPV case remains conditional on validated performance, eligibility, servicing, legal transfer, structural protection, and the approved financial model.
