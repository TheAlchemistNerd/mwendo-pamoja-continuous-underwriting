How can high-frequency telematics contribute credible, explainable evidence to an actuarial tariff while the same predictive distribution supports claims reserving, economic capital and risk-transfer decisions?

I explore this question in my new white paper:

“Bayesian Credibility and Exposure-Normalised Telematics Relativities”

The paper begins with a practical observation: two gig-economy drivers can operate similar vehicles in the same city yet create materially different insurance risks.

One may work predictable daytime routes. Another may drive longer night shifts through unfamiliar corridors, navigate volatile demand patterns and continue operating while fatigued because household cash flow depends on completing one more trip.

A conventional tariff can recognise vehicle class, territory, driver characteristics and claims history. Telematics adds something different: evidence about how exposure and operating conditions evolve between traditional rating observations.

The actuarial challenge is not simply whether a neural network can predict claims. It is how to convert high-frequency evidence into quantities that remain technically sound, interpretable, calibrated and governable.

The paper develops an architecture in which:

- Exposure is measured explicitly and remains separate from the claim-rate parameter.
- Claim frequency and conditional severity are modelled as distinct processes.
- Interpretable telematics variables and neural sequence representations have clearly assigned roles.
- Neural embeddings are cross-fitted and residualised against explicit variables to reduce redundant representation.
- Regularised horseshoe priors control the remaining embedding contribution.
- Hierarchical Bayesian credibility allows individual experience to accumulate without discarding portfolio knowledge.
- Expected-loss-weighted calibration preserves the approved tariff foundation.
- Temporary safety interventions remain separate from persistent premium decisions.
- The predictive loss distribution continues into RBNS, IBNR and IBNER reserve estimates.
- One-year and ultimate loss views support economic-capital assessment.
- Quota-share, excess-of-loss and aggregate protection are priced and monitored against gross and net loss distributions.
- Event-time processing, point-in-time joins and versioned model artefacts preserve reproducibility and auditability.

The central proposal is an exposure-normalised actuarial modulating variable: a calibrated mechanism that redistributes expected driving loss within an approved tariff population without silently replacing the portfolio foundation.

This creates a continuous chain:

Driver activity → validated exposure → explicit and learned risk evidence → hierarchical frequency-severity modelling → calibrated premium relativity → claim-development distribution → reserves → economic capital → risk-transfer monitoring.

The broader objective is to connect innovative Insurtech product design with established actuarial disciplines. Predictive accuracy matters, but so do exposure integrity, uncertainty, credibility, fairness, claim cash flows, capital consumption, reinsurance performance and the ability to explain each material decision.
