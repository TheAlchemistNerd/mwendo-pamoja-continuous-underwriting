# IEEE Annotated Source Index

## Index protocol

This index records every distinct URL found in the three `question drafts` files, including inaccessible, weak, duplicate-purpose, and non-substantive links. It also adds primary sources needed to adjudicate the documents. URLs were accessed on 24 August 2026. Tracking parameters were removed where they did not identify content; the YouTube timestamp is retained in its occurrence note. Tier 1 is a regulator, law, standard setter, official methodology, or original vendor specification. Tier 2 is peer-reviewed or established academic research. Tier 3 is professional commentary, vendor explanation, or established media. Tier 4 is social, marketing, aggregator, search artefact, or unverifiable material. `QD-ALM` denotes *Asset Liability and Mature Funds Transfer Pricing*; `QD-RN` denotes *risk neutral measure, KESONIA compounding, WACF*; `QD-Delta` denotes the dual Delta Lake draft.

## Tier 1: official and primary sources

### [1] Central Bank of Kenya, revised risk-based credit pricing model

Central Bank of Kenya, “Revised Risk-Based Credit Pricing Model,” Aug. 2025. [Online]. Available: https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Revised-Risk-based-Credit-Pricing-Model.pdf. [Accessed: Aug. 24, 2026].

- **Access:** Accessible PDF; supplemental primary source.
- **Summary:** The framework sets KESONIA as the common reference rate for variable-rate Kenya shilling credit and defines the customer premium `K` by reference to lending-related costs, shareholder return, and borrower risk. It requires transparency and consistency rather than reducing `K` to expected loss. It separates the lending rate from fees and charges used to disclose total cost. It supports D01, D03, D07, and D09 corrections on customer pricing, benchmark scope, disclosure, and governance.
- **Does not support:** A separate generic bank margin after `K`, a mandatory five-day lookback, a particular SPV note coupon, or any claim that a Bayesian PD is itself the regulatory premium.
- **Limitations:** Applies within its stated Kenyan bank-credit scope and effective dates. Definitive CBK text and lender contracts prevail over summaries.

### [2] Central Bank of Kenya, issuance notice for revised pricing model

Central Bank of Kenya, “Issuance of a Revised Risk-Based Credit Pricing Model,” press release, Aug. 2025. [Online]. Available: https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/2044081907_Press%20Release%20-%20Issuance%20of%20a%20Revised%20Risk-Based%20Credit%20Pricing%20Model.pdf. [Accessed: Aug. 24, 2026].

- **Access:** Accessible PDF; supplemental primary source.
- **Summary:** The notice gives the concise formulas: total lending rate equals KESONIA plus premium `K`, while total cost adds fees and charges. It records 1 September 2025 for new variable-rate loans and 28 February 2026 for existing facilities. It describes CBR as an alternative in specified circumstances and excludes fixed-rate and foreign-currency facilities from the variable KES formulation.
- **Does not support:** Calling KESONIA risk free, automatic daily repricing of existing customers, or applying the customer formula to the SPV note waterfall.
- **Relevant findings:** D01-I04, D03-I03, D07-I06, D09-I01 through D09-I04.

### [3] Central Bank of Kenya, KESONIA information page

Central Bank of Kenya, “Kenya Shilling Overnight Interbank Average (KESONIA),” [Online]. Available: https://www.centralbank.go.ke/kesonia/. [Accessed: Aug. 24, 2026].

- **Origin:** QD-RN.
- **Access:** Accessible.
- **Summary:** CBK describes KESONIA as a transaction-based volume-weighted average for overnight unsecured Kenya shilling interbank transactions. CBK administers and publishes the rate for business days; the last business-day rate applies on non-publication days. The page provides benchmark notices and supporting publications. It is the controlling source for the benchmark's administrator, economic basis, and publication practice.
- **Does not support:** A universal observation lookback, a SOFR-style compounding convention, or a claim that KESONIA eliminates credit and liquidity risk in every contract.
- **Limitations:** Product-specific accrual and fallback mechanics still require contract definitions.

### [4] IFRS Foundation, IBOR Reform, Phase 2

IFRS Foundation, “Interest Rate Benchmark Reform, Phase 2,” amendments to IFRS 9, IAS 39, IFRS 7, IFRS 4 and IFRS 16, Aug. 2020. [Online]. Available: https://www.ifrs.org/projects/completed-projects/2020/ibor-reform-and-its-effects-on-financial-reporting-phase-2/. [Accessed: Aug. 24, 2026].

- **Access:** Accessible; supplemental primary source.
- **Summary:** The amendments address accounting effects when an existing benchmark is replaced by an alternative benchmark as a direct consequence of reform and on an economically equivalent basis. They provide targeted relief for contractual cash-flow changes, hedge accounting, and disclosures. This is narrower than a general permission to reset any loan's effective interest rate without modification analysis.
- **Does not support:** D09's claim that every routine KESONIA reset receives an IBOR Phase 2 expedient or that commercial repricing of `K_RBCP` is automatically non-substantial.
- **Relevant findings:** D07-I05 and D09-I09.

### [5] IFRS Foundation, IFRS 9 Financial Instruments

IFRS Foundation, “IFRS 9 Financial Instruments,” [Online]. Available: https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-9-financial-instruments/. [Accessed: Aug. 24, 2026].

- **Access:** Accessible overview; complete standard may require licensed access.
- **Summary:** IFRS 9 governs classification and measurement, impairment using expected credit losses, and hedge accounting for financial instruments. ### [6] IFRS Foundation, IFRS 17 Insurance Contracts

- **Access:** Accessible overview; supplemental primary source.

### [7] Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, IRRBB
