# Mwendo Pamoja: proposed DCP positioning and peer research leads

**Positioning discussion retained from the 22 September 2026 review; editorial status clarified 28 September 2026.**

This is a research and structuring note. It does not establish that Mwendo holds a DCP licence or that the proposed SPV, insurance arrangements or financing terms are executed. Named organisations and reported facilities are comparator leads from the earlier discussion. The licensing/status assertions and source labels below still require dated primary-source links before reuse in public copy or a regulatory submission; they have not been freshly verified by this cleanup. Current controlled specifications own programme and transaction assumptions.

The source discussion is preserved in the local pending-resolution archive. Its historical conversational instructions are not current instructions to change neighbouring projects.

The organisations below belong to a shared strategic reference class; regulatory category, product structure and maturity must be assessed separately.
Mwendo Pamoja’s intended centre is:
A proposed DCP-led insurtech, fintech and embedded-finance platform, subject to the required licensing and approvals, that originates and services credit, integrates insurance through licensed carriers, uses platform and telematics evidence, and finances eligible receivables through institutional facilities and a ring-fenced SPV.

A DCP licence permits credit provision and associated approved activities, but it does not permit deposit-taking. The insurance component must also preserve the responsibilities of licensed insurers and authorised distribution arrangements. Kenya’s DCP Regulations therefore define Mwendo’s regulatory centre without turning it into a bank, microfinance bank or insurer.
The correct relationship with Underwrite for Collection is:
- Underwrite for Collection is an institution-neutral lifecycle-credit framework. It applies to banks, microfinance banks, DCPs, fintech lenders, embedded-finance providers, asset-finance companies and receivables vehicles.
- Its bank-controlled sandbox and prudential treatment represent the most demanding institutional implementation. They do not redefine the framework as a banking or microfinance paper.
- Mwendo Pamoja is a specific DCP-led application of that framework for gig-economy mobility, embedded insurance, continuous underwriting and SPV-funded receivables.

Mwendo remains differentiated because it proposes a multi-product mobility architecture spanning:
- Insurance premium finance.
- Microloans.
- Revolving credit.
- Telematics and wallet evidence.
- Continuous Bayesian underwriting.
- Intervention before default.
- Insurance-carrier integration.
- Platform relationships.
- Multi-product contagion management.
- A dedicated SPV capital stack.
The companies named above should be described as precedents and comparators, not as proof that every element of Mwendo’s architecture is already legally or commercially validated.
For the future Mwendo Part 5 plan, the institutional structure should be recast around five participants:
1. Mwendo DCP entity: originator, credit decision-maker, product owner and regulated lender.
2. Mwendo technology and servicing layer: data processing, underwriting infrastructure, customer interfaces, collections and portfolio administration.
3. Licensed insurance carriers and authorised intermediaries: insurance underwriting, premium receipt, policy administration, cancellations, claims and eligible refunds.
4. Banks, microfinance banks, DFIs and institutional funders: warehouse funding, account infrastructure, guarantees, risk participation and senior capital.
5. Ring-fenced SPV and transaction parties: receivable purchases, controlled accounts, reserves, notes, waterfalls, covenants and investor reporting.
This preserves the $9 million SPV and its specific Class A, B and C values while preventing bank-level ALM, deposit FTP, LCR, NSFR and IRRBB from being attributed directly to Mwendo’s DCP entity. Those mechanisms remain relevant where they affect a bank or microfinance-bank funding partner.
The evidence architecture should likewise distinguish:
- Direct legal authority: Kenyan legislation, CBK, CMA and IRA sources.
- General lifecycle methodology: Underwrite for Collection and peer-reviewed credit-risk literature.
- Commercial precedent: M-KOPA, SunCulture, Sun King and d.light.
- Regional comparison: Branch and Moniepoint.
- Transaction assumptions: Mwendo’s facility size, tranche structure, advance rate, reserves and waterfall.
That framing protects both papers. Underwrite for Collection remains a general credit architecture, while Mwendo Pamoja becomes a focused DCP-led platform and financing proposition informed by mature African fintech and PAYG precedents.
