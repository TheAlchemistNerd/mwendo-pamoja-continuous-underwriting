A cash-flow claim becomes meaningful when a lender can show how it measured repayment capacity, matched payments to a business cycle and learned from what happened after disbursement.

My new article applies the discussion around Moniepoint’s cash-flow lending position to Underwrite for Collection and Kenya’s digital credit environment. It distinguishes the October reporting from Tosin Eniolorunda’s separate June post on adding credit to payment flows.

The article includes a worked merchant cash-flow case, distinguishes financing an operating business from financing a ring-fenced receivables stream, and sets out a governed agentic workflow with reproducible calculations, audit trails and meaningful human review. It also examines Kenya’s 2026 non-deposit-taking credit framework and data-protection requirements.

Responses to Tosin’s June payment-data post raised practical questions about infrastructure and portability, access for businesses beyond major urban centres, and responsible use of transaction data. Those are useful implementation questions for any lender building from payment activity.
